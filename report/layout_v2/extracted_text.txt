Sequence-aware m6A site prediction and cautious exploration
across SG-NEx cell lines
Introduction
N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is an RNA modification that can be investigated using nanopore direct-RNA
sequencing. Electrical-event measurements depend on local sequence as well as modification state,
complicating prediction. We developed a site-supervised model and applied one frozen pipeline to
SG-NEx data [1,2]. Our aim was reproducible candidate prioritisation with transparent limitations, rather
than interpreting model scores as measured modification fractions.
Problem statement
Each candidate site contains a seven-base context and a variable-sized bag of reads, each represented
by nine measurements: dwell time, event standard deviation (SD) and mean current at three neighbouring
events. Labels describe sites, not individual reads. The tasks were to assess site-label prediction and
compare predicted patterns across cell lines without confusing missing coverage, technical differences or
training exposure with biological differences.
Methods
Data and exploratory rationale. Training data0 comprised 121,838 sites, 5,475 positives and 3,852
genes. Data1 contained 90,810 sites and 6,593 positives; data2 contained 1,323 rows with fractional
labels and repeated sequence templates. Only data0 was used for the selected pipeline's fitting and
tuning. Data1 had already informed earlier development; data2 was not converted into binary labels.
Exploration showed that pooled central current increased from 114 to 118 between data0 label groups,
although positives had lower current within 11/15 adequately supported motifs. This motivated sequence
conditioning and distribution summaries. Data0/data1 shared 67,320 literal site keys, including 3,872 label
disagreements, supporting cautious transfer interpretation. Valid unusual sites were retained.
Evaluation and model. Early screening included logistic regression, support vector machines, random
forest, XGBoost and LightGBM, using 18 read-mean/SD features and shared gene folds. Tuning budgets
and verification depth differed; their historical data1-exposed results are not merged into the later
comparison.
The later assessment retained the exact five data0 gene folds. For outer fold f, inner fold (f+1) mod 5
selected training duration; fresh weights and training-only normalisation then fitted all four
non-assessment folds before scoring f. Every gene's sites remained together. Ranking used area under
the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and precision-recall (PR) curves (AUC). Primary score S was
the unweighted mean of five (ROC AUC + trapezoidal PR AUC)/2 values. Average precision and pooled
out-of-fold areas were separate summaries. Repeated development on these folds limits their
interpretation as generalisation estimates.
The selected neural pipeline combined seven-base embeddings, width-48 read encoding, gated
attention/mean/SD pooling and 63 all-read distribution summaries. Training sampled at most 128 reads
per site, with positive loss weight three; prediction used all valid reads. No probability calibrator was fitted.
A fresh 18-epoch model was fitted on all data0, using the median inner-selected duration, and frozen
before the reported data1 diagnostic and SG-NEx application.

Results (1): model evaluation
All benchmark rows covered the same 121,838 assessment sites. Logistic regression was a fresh
fixed-recipe baseline; LightGBM and the neural model used gene-held-out development fits. Published
m6Anet used pretrained HCT116_RNA002 weights: fold IDs stratified its predictions, rather than defining
retraining partitions [1].

The neural-m6Anet S difference was approximately 0.000998; m6Anet had higher ROC AUC. Unresolved
pretraining overlap prevents an independent-superiority claim. Increasing the neural training read cap
from 32 to 128 increased S from 0.717642 to 0.718715; an added relative-context branch decreased it to
0.708885. Removing positive weighting gave 0.718381 and reduced recall at 0.5. These small
development differences do not establish statistical significance.
A separate external neural trial genuinely achieved higher S, 0.726644, improving all five folds. Promotion
remained inconclusive: fixed-cutoff/subgroup outcomes were mixed, evidence used one seed and
different computational conditions, and its final general prediction interface/artifact was incomplete. At
200+ reads, recall was 151/461 versus 168/461 for cap128 despite better ranking. Retaining the
integrated pipeline was a conservative tradeoff, not proof it was globally best.
Frozen data1 ROC/PR/S were 0.827381/0.382350/0.604866, versus LightGBM
0.821764/0.382879/0.602322. Neural recall/F1 were higher at 0.5, whereas LightGBM had higher
precision/specificity. This exposed transfer diagnostic did not reselect the model. Final all-data0
predictions were fitted-data outputs, not validation; no binary data2 AUC was claimed.

Results (2): application to SG-NEx data
SG-NEx comparison. We analysed 23 processed runs spanning seven cell lines and 17 metadata
groups. Sites required at least 20 reads for prediction. Matching retained literal transcript positions, explicit
per-run aliases and the model's uppercase/U-to-T encoding. Primary comparisons required consistent
contexts, annotated human keys and at least two eligible metadata groups per line. Eligible runs were
averaged within group, then groups received equal weight. Pairwise and all-seven common populations
were separate. Read thresholds 20/50/100 and score cutoffs 0.5/0.7/0.9 were descriptive sensitivities.
The unchanged model produced 2,983,948 scored run-site observations using 232,253,678 reads.
Context auditing compared 8,780,071 source observations, including low-read records, at 451,352
scoreable keys: zero context conflicts or exclusions occurred. Another 19,890,009 observations outside
that union were counted but not cross-run context-compared. Agreement does not verify historical
reference identity or event offsets.
Requiring two groups per line yielded 33,452 all-seven common sites at 20 reads, 11,560 at 50 and 5,058
at 100. On the same 33,452-site panel, mean scores ranged from 0.0705 to 0.0946; 3.76-5.20% met 0.5
and only 15-31 sites per line met 0.9. Hct116 had the highest mean but was also the training cell line.
These fractions describe predictions, not modification prevalence.
Median run-pair Spearman agreement at 20 reads was 0.7904 across six same-group pairs, 0.7934
across 28 other-group same-line pairs and 0.7815 across 219 cross-line pairs. Overlapping ranges and
shared sequence structure limit a cell-line-separation interpretation. Each correlation uses its own
matched sites. Across all 21 line pairs, the exploratory screen required absolute mean-score difference
≥0.2, separated group ranges and coverage ratio ≤2. For example, 2,331/120,195 Hct116-HepG2 shared
sites passed; no significance/false-discovery control was applied. All outcomes and valid extremes were
retained.

Discussion
The pipeline provides useful site-label ranking and an inspectable SG-NEx resource, with substantial
limits. Among 33,452 common sites, 22,219 had fewer than 20 positive training labels for their seven-base
context, including 4,290 with none; all remained with warnings. Reused development folds, exposed
data1, uncertain pretrained overlap and one training seed restrict generalisation claims.
Basecaller/platform/date differences are confounded with cell line; metadata groups are not verified
independent laboratory preparations. Nested depth sensitivities and high score agreement cannot resolve
those explanations. Independent compatible assays and verified reference/offset mappings are needed
before chemical, stoichiometric or causal interpretation.
Code Availability
Code, the frozen model, example and reproduction instructions are available at LIFO_m6A_project.
Hosted explorer: SG-NEx m6A Explorer. The hosted explorer retains all 23 runs and literal case links.
Hosted case controls, click/reload navigation and an original-score CSV download passed; anonymous
file checks preserved the original data. Native AWS example parity passed; this establishes software
portability, not unseen accuracy. Actual new-test input/submission remains separate and unclaimed.
